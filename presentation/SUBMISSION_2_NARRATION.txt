Team Kremsians built an Industrial AI system for semiconductor process logic.
The input is a partial process sequence. The output is three official files:
nextstep dot csv, completion dot csv, and anomaly dot csv.

For next-step prediction and completion, we trained a GPT decoder from scratch
on process-step tokens. This is not a generic LLM wrapper. One fab step is one
token, and the model is optimized directly for the unit the scorer evaluates.

For anomaly detection, the process rules are explicit, so we use a deterministic
validator. It returns validity, a score, and the official violated rule name.

The key evaluation choice is out-of-distribution testing. We use fifteen
families total: the three organizer families plus twelve synthetic process
families. The benchmark trains on twelve families and tests on three held-out
families, then reports the family-wise average. No official eval sequence is
used as training data.

On the held-out families, the trained GPT improves next-step Top-1 from zero
point six six two to zero point seven two two, and Top-5 from zero point nine
seven six to zero point nine nine nine. On the official-scorer self-eval split,
completion normalized edit distance drops from zero point three three six to
zero point two six three. For anomaly detection, F1, ROC-AUC, and rule
attribution are all one point zero zero.

The same-input demo shows the difference. On a MOSFET prefix, the n-gram
baseline ranks STRIP PHOTORESIST second. The trained GPT ranks the exact
ground-truth next step first.

The repository contains the checkpoint, loss curve, score reports, official
format CSVs, and exact commands. It runs on an Apple Silicon MacBook with MPS,
or on Leonardo with CUDA using the included SLURM job.
